# Data-Driven Insights & Actionable Recommendations

This document translates raw analytical findings from our SQL queries and BI dashboards into strategic business insights, concluding with structured recommendations for senior leadership.

---

## Key Findings (Attrition Drivers)

### 1. The Burnout Factor: Overtime Work
* **Insight**: Employees who work overtime exhibit an attrition rate that is **more than double** the rate of those who do not work overtime. 
* **Combined Risk**: Overtime workers who also receive a low annual salary hike (<15%) represent the highest-risk group in the company, with attrition levels exceeding 60%. This indicates a strong correlation between workload intensity, lack of financial appreciation, and employee resignation.

### 2. Compensation Stagnation
* **Insight**: Turnover is heavily concentrated in the lower income brackets. Employees earning **under $3,000 per month** show a turnover rate far exceeding the corporate average.
* **Hike Correlation**: A clear trend reveals that annual salary increases below 15% fail to act as retention incentives. Turnover drops significantly once salary hikes exceed 18–20%.

### 3. Role-Specific Turnover Pockets
* **Insight**: Attrition is not uniform across the company. The roles of **Sales Executives, Laboratory Technicians, and Research Scientists** account for over 50% of the total attrition count. 
* **Departmental Impact**: The Sales and Research & Development (R&D) departments represent the most volatile units.

### 4. Career Progression & Onboarding Stagnation
* **Insight**: Employees are most vulnerable to turnover during their first 24 months. New joiners (0-2 years) have a high turnover rate, which points to potential gaps in initial expectations, role alignment, or onboarding support.
* **Promotion Stagnation**: A spike in attrition occurs among employees who have spent 1–4 years in their current role without a promotion, demonstrating that a lack of visible vertical movement drives exits.

### 5. Self-Reported Satisfaction Scores
* **Insight**: The self-reported metrics (Job Satisfaction and Environment Satisfaction) are highly reliable leading indicators of resignation. Employees reporting scores of 1 or 2 (on a 1–4 scale) are three times more likely to leave than those reporting 3 or 4.

---

## Actionable Recommendations

### 1. Overtime Cap & Burnout Prevention
* **Action**: Implement a departmental threshold on monthly overtime hours. If a role requires consistent overtime, mandate a workload review to evaluate if additional headcount is necessary.
* **HR Intervention**: Require managers to check in on employees working more than 10 hours of overtime per week, focusing on R&D and Sales roles.

### 2. Target Compensation Adjustment for Key Roles
* **Action**: Conduct a market compensation review for low-earning brackets (under $3,000/month) and high-attrition roles (Sales Executives, Lab Technicians).
* **Raise Alignment**: Standardize annual hikes such that high performers receive a minimum 15% raise, directly linking compensation growth to individual contributions.

### 3. Career Pathing & Mentorship Programs
* **Action**: Establish formal, biannual career pathing conversations for employees at the 12-month and 24-month tenure marks.
* **Internal Mobility**: Create clear lateral and vertical transition paths to ensure mid-tenure employees see a long-term future at the organization without having to change companies to advance.

### 4. Leadership & Relationship Training for Managers
* **Action**: Since a significant percentage of departing employees leave during their first few years with a manager, introduce mandatory manager-employee relationship guidelines, including structured feedback loops and conflict resolution training for team leads.
